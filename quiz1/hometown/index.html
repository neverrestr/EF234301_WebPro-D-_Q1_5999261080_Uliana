My Hometown — Moscow

A City of Contrasts

Moscow is the capital of Russia, its largest city, and the biggest metropolis in Europe. It's a place where a 16th-century fortress sits beside glass skyscrapers, where the metro feels like a museum, and where more than 40% of the territory is still green.

I've lived here long enough to stop counting the landmarks and start noticing the everyday details — the light in winter, the pace of the boulevards in summer, the way the city rearranges itself around each season. Below are five facts I'd tell anyone visiting for the first time.

Five Facts About Moscow

1. Europe's Largest City

Moscow is home to over 12.6 million people — more than London. Roughly 11% of Russia's entire population lives here.

2. The Kremlin Fortress

The Moscow Kremlin is the largest still-functioning fortress in Europe. Together with Red Square, it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

3. The Metro as a Museum

The Moscow Metro is one of the most beautiful in the world — some stations are protected cultural heritage sites. It is also the fifth-longest metro system on the planet.

4. Europe's Largest Library

The Russian State Library is the national library of Russia and the largest in Europe — one of the biggest collections of books anywhere in the world.

5. A Very Green Megacity

Over 40% of Moscow is green space. In the Kolomenskoye museum-reserve, some oak trees are more than 700 years old.

Why I Love It

  • Walkability — the center is best explored on foot, one boulevard at a time.
  • History on every block — from medieval walls to Soviet modernism to brand-new districts.
  • Four real seasons — snowy winters, bright springs, warm summers, golden autumns.
  • Culture everywhere — theaters, museums, galleries, and libraries within reach.
